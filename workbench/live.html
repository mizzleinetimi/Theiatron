What these measurements mean
  • Boxes and IDs are fresh model output. The displayed object count is visible detections, not unique vehicles or calibrated traffic flow.
  • The input buffer holds one pending frame. Slow inference overwrites old pending frames; frames already over 300 ms old are rejected before inference. Drops and deadline misses remain visible.
  • p50/p95 cover this session, using 1 ms histogram upper bounds. Processing FPS covers the last 100 observations. Preview delivery is separate from inference latency.
  • After a loop or a source-time gap over 0.5 seconds, the tracker starts a new identity namespace. No trajectory is invented across that gap.
  • The 3D view projects fresh detector anchors onto this clip’s saved road and camera estimates. It does not read saved vehicle tracks. Camera transforms must match the exact source bytes and native frame; missing or rejected mappings hide cars. A different sample cadence may lack matching camera frames. Try the original analysis cadence (10 FPS for IMG_0635).
  • Road geometry and camera-motion compensation are reused, not estimated live. Vehicle sizes are nominal; colours identify tracks, not paint. Heading comes from observed motion and is initially unknown. Causal position filtering and up to 120 ms visual easing reduce jitter; they add display lag and are not accuracy validation. Missing detections, jumps, stale observations and stopped sessions hide cars without extrapolation. The view does not feed SUMO.
  • Sessions stop at the chosen time limit. Stopping keeps the model warm; restarting the service ends its worker. A separate explicit RTSP CLI adapter exists, but has not been field-tested.
